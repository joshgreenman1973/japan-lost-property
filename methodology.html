How this was built

The source, the translation, the year system, and the large hole in the middle of this subject.

The source

All figures come from one page: the Tokyo Metropolitan Police Department's annual lost-property statistics, 遺失物取扱状況, last updated March 2, 2026. The tables there are HTML, not a data file, so the build script parses them directly and fails loudly if the expected tables are not present.

The prefecture directory comes from the National Police Agency's index of prefectural lost-property searches. All 47 are listed; 43 of them point at one shared national database and four run their own site.

The year

Japanese government statistics use imperial era years. This data covers 令和7年, Reiwa 7, which is the calendar year 2025. The comparison column throughout is 令和6年, Reiwa 6, or 2024. Reiwa 1 was 2019.

Two different counts that must not be added together

The police publish two separate series and this site keeps them apart:

  • 拾得, things found and handed in. 4,538,244 reports covering 4,850,775 items and ¥4,508,824,075 in cash.
  • 遺失, things people came to report losing. 1,100,657 reports covering 2,291,811 items and ¥8,325,160,470 in cash.

These are counts of different events and there is no key joining them. The "found versus reported lost" view puts them side by side to show an asymmetry; it is not a matching rate, and you cannot subtract one from the other to get a number of unreturned items. More cash is reported lost than is handed in, which is what you would expect and is not evidence of anything.

Why the return rates are trustworthy and the totals are trickier

The disposition table is internally consistent: for each category, the four outcomes sum to that category's total. Return rates are computed straight from it. But that table's grand total, 4,750,389 items, differs slightly from the headline item count of 4,850,775, because the disposition table covers items processed in the year and the headline covers items received. Items received in late December are processed the following year. Do not mix the two.

What is missing, and it is a lot

  • The other 46 prefectures. Only Tokyo publishes this depth in a form that can be parsed. The prefecture tab links to each official search, but there is no national item-level dataset behind it.
  • Item-level records. The searchable police databases are session-based Japanese-language forms with no bulk export and no API. Nothing here is item-level; it is all category totals.
  • History. This is one year against the prior year. The police page does not publish a long machine-readable series.
  • Anything found and kept. By definition this counts only what someone handed to the police.

Rebuilding it

build_japan.py in this repository fetches both pages and writes data/data.json. It exits non-zero if the Tokyo page's table structure changes. The police update the page once a year, in early March.

Built with AI assistance. Figures are parsed from the police tables rather than retyped, but the source page is the authority.
